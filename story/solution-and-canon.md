# The solution and the canon

**Host and editor eyes only. This gives the whole mystery away.**

This is the case as the cards, the evidence and the finale assume it. Edit a card only in a way that keeps these facts true. The rules for writing around them are in [../docs/design-principles.md](../docs/design-principles.md).

## The case in one paragraph

**Veronica Vane**, a former soap star whom Vincent Vale signed at eighteen and who has paid him half of every fee since, was about to make a comeback in a new BBC drama. Vale's contract gave him a personal right to half her future performance fees, and he was about to publicise a recording that would expose it to her new employer. She asked **Dr. Beverly Steele** for a sample, saying Vale wanted to inspect it. **Scooter** delivered it, sealed in a silver compact marked VV. Veronica bought herself twenty seconds of music and stage-lighting distraction, paid **Dakota Steele** to keep quiet about the compact, and emptied the sample into Vale's private wine decanter while the music played. Vale drank it at the toast and collapsed.

## Who did what

**The distraction and the payments.** Veronica requested the twenty-second music distraction and paid Dakota for her silence.

- **Leo Lights** directly heard Veronica's request and saw her leave a deep-red envelope at Spinny's empty desk.
- **Glenda Gold** independently saw Veronica put the other deep-red envelope into Dakota's open coat pocket while Dakota faced the attendant.
- Spinny and Dakota did not see who sent theirs. Their matching paper stock suggests a link; the eyewitnesses identify the deliveries, and the slips establish what the payments bought. No office order or clerk's carbon copy is needed.

**The sample.** Beverly supplied an *undiluted multi-dose* stimulant formulation in a sealed silver compact marked VV, and warned Veronica directly that swallowing the whole sample could be fatal. Scooter delivered it sealed to Veronica; Dakota saw it delivered. Veronica emptied it into Vale's private decanter. **The fatal wine is separate from the general champagne and from the separate dish.**

**The separate dish.** Beverly separately added a non-lethal sedative to a dish meant for Vale, to stop him speaking. **Cassie Chase** withdrew it because its cover and seal were disturbed. It never reached him. It is a serious red herring, not the murder.

**The laboratory.** The report the Inspector hands out says: the residue in the compact (C1) and the wine from Vale's decanter and last glass (W1) are the same batch of stimulant; the medical findings show the amount swallowed in the wine was fatal; the dish (D1) holds a *different* non-lethal sedative; the fabric swab (F1) is consistent with the stimulant in C1 and W1. **Fiona Fringe** identifies F1 as a swab from Veronica's gown. The trace cannot date the contact or say who was wearing the gown at that moment. The chemistry supports a connection; it does not give a unique container direction, a culprit or a contamination time.

**The sightings.** **Nikki Neon** identifies Veronica approaching the drinks; **Dakota** and **Ronnie Reels** independently identify her at the decanter. Each has a defined view and an obstruction, and **nobody sees an unobstructed transfer of powder**. Vance's photograph shows a hand near the neck of the stopper, not falling powder. Motive, informed access, independent sightings and the envelope deliveries must be weighed together: no single exhibit proves guilt.

**The motive.** An older recording of an exchange (called VV-1 on the cards, Vale's own label for it) establishes Veronica's fee dispute. **Brenda Blip** heard it live; **Tommy Tech** and **Barry Byte** have older copies, not current gala footage. **Peter Vale** reviewed the fictional contract: Vale's personal right to half Veronica's future performance fees ends on his death, but accrued estate debts remain. The publicity would have exposed that existing right to her new BBC employer and threatened the comeback; it did not create the agreement. This is a supplied fictional term, not a general legal rule.

**The tape that never played.** Vale intended to release a recording of Veronica admitting she signed immediately after the toast, and **Sabrina Sinclair** had been told to run the story afterwards under the title *Comeback Girl*. The recording never played. **Colin Cable** checked the separate local playback after the collapse: armed, no "go" entered, and no remote cancellation.

## Witness limits

Locations concern the stated intervals only, not every earlier chance to reach the wine. Do not make an innocent character prove a complete alibi where none is supplied.

- Cassie and Beverly corroborate one another at the service hatch from 19:28:45 to the alarm. No service supervisor's testimony is supplied.
- Larry saw Rex on passes at 19:28:50 and 19:29:05, then stayed: keep the gap between them.
- Spinny continuously watched Ricky outside the doorway through cue and alarm.
- Johnny continuously watched Robbie in the lobby from 19:28 to the alarm. That does not establish where Sid was.
- Frankie and Lola corroborate each other; that is not an independent alibi.
- The taxi-driver note corroborates Peter's *journey* only; his appointment, and his reading of the clause, are his own account.
- Other histories stay as supplied, including Ronnie Redhand's Soviet contacts, without an invented operational assignment.

## What is deliberately not in the story

No solicitor, clerk or duty-manager telephone calls; no printed transcript, contract bundle, office order, passport (apart from Tina's), lobby or reception photograph sets, or jacket note; no real substances; no restraint of any kind. The Round 4 closing sequence keeps the confession withheld until the conclusion.
